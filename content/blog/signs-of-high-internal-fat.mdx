Internal fat sits deep in the abdomen, which means you often can't see it directly — you can look reasonably fit and still be carrying more of it than is ideal. These seven signs are the patterns worth paying attention to, individually and especially in combination.

## Why Internal Fat Can Be Invisible From the Outside

Unlike the fat you can pinch under your skin, internal fat wraps around your abdominal organs, beneath the muscle wall. Someone can have a relatively flat stomach and still carry a meaningful amount of it — a pattern often called "thin outside, fat inside," or TOFI. This is exactly why relying on appearance or even your overall weight alone can miss what's actually going on beneath the surface. The signs below are more reliable clues than a glance in the mirror.

## The 7 Signs

### 1. A waist measurement that's grown, even if your weight hasn't

Internal fat tends to show up in waist size before it shows up on the bathroom scale. If your clothes are fitting tighter around the middle specifically, while your overall weight has stayed fairly stable, that's a meaningful pattern worth noting — not something to dismiss just because the scale looks unchanged.

### 2. A firm, rounded abdomen rather than a soft one

This isn't a diagnostic rule, but many people describe internal fat as giving the stomach a tighter, more domed quality — sometimes compared to early pregnancy — as opposed to the softer feel of subcutaneous fat elsewhere on the body.

### 3. Energy crashes an hour or two after meals

A pattern of feeling energised right after eating, followed by a slump, brain fog, or sudden hunger within a couple of hours, often reflects the blood sugar swings associated with insulin resistance — which is closely tied to internal fat.

### 4. Strong cravings for sugar or refined carbohydrates

Particularly in the afternoon or evening, persistent cravings that feel disproportionate to actual hunger are frequently a downstream effect of those same blood sugar fluctuations, rather than a simple lack of discipline.

### 5. Fatigue that doesn't match your sleep

Feeling tired despite what should be adequate sleep can reflect chronically elevated insulin and blood sugar, which interfere with how efficiently your body uses energy, even when rest itself is sufficient.

### 6. Darker or thicker patches of skin in body folds

A condition called acanthosis nigricans — darker, slightly velvety skin, often at the neck, armpits, or other folds — is associated with insulin resistance. It's not always present, but when it is, it's worth specifically mentioning to a doctor.

### 7. Weight that concentrates around the midsection

Even without significant overall weight gain, fat that seems to preferentially accumulate around the waist rather than the hips, thighs, or arms is a pattern worth noting — it often reflects a higher proportion of internal versus subcutaneous fat relative to your total body composition.

## How Many Signs Should Concern You?

One sign in isolation, especially something like occasional afternoon tiredness, isn't cause for alarm — plenty of things can explain a single symptom on its own. It's the combination and persistence that matters. If three or more of these signs are consistently present, especially waist growth alongside energy crashes or cravings, it's a reasonable enough pattern to take seriously and worth exploring further, either through the lifestyle changes covered below or a conversation with your doctor.

It's also worth noting that these signs can overlap with other conditions — thyroid issues, for example, can produce similar fatigue and weight patterns, which is covered in our [thyroid and weight gain guide](/blog/thyroid-and-weight-gain-lifestyle-guide) — so persistent symptoms are always worth a proper conversation with a doctor rather than self-diagnosis based on a list like this one.

## What to Do If You Notice These Signs

Recognising the pattern is the useful first step, but it's what happens next that actually matters.

**Start tracking waist size, not just weight.** A simple tape measure, checked monthly rather than daily, gives you a more relevant trend line than the scale alone.

**Look at meal composition before anything else.** Meals built around protein and fibre, rather than carbohydrates alone, produce a gentler insulin response — often the single highest-leverage change available.

**Add a short walk after your largest meal.** This is one of the most consistently effective, lowest-effort habits for improving insulin sensitivity, and it's realistic to do daily.

**Prioritise sleep and stress as seriously as food.** Both directly affect insulin sensitivity, and neither gets the attention it deserves in most weight-focused advice.

**Talk to your doctor if signs persist or you have risk factors.** A family history of diabetes, thyroid disease, or unexplained symptoms are all good reasons to get an actual blood test rather than relying on signs alone.

## Signs That Are Often Confused With Internal Fat

A few common experiences get attributed to internal fat when something else may be at play, which is worth untangling before assuming the worst.

**General tiredness from a busy season of life** — a demanding work period, a new baby, disrupted travel — can mimic the fatigue described above without internal fat being the driver at all. Context matters; a single stressful month is a different picture than a persistent, unexplained pattern over many months.

**Bloating alone**, without the other signs, is more often related to digestion and eating habits than internal fat specifically — our guide on [why bloating happens](/blog/why-am-i-always-bloated) covers this in more detail.

**Occasional sugar cravings** — during PMS, after a poor night's sleep, or around a stressful event — are normal and don't necessarily indicate a chronic pattern on their own. It's the frequency and persistence that distinguishes an occasional craving from a sign worth tracking.

This distinction matters because it's easy to over-interpret a single data point. The goal of this list isn't to create anxiety about ordinary variation — it's to help you notice a genuine, persistent pattern if one exists.

## Summary

Internal fat often shows up through indirect signs — waist growth, post-meal energy crashes, persistent cravings, fatigue, or skin changes — rather than obvious visible weight gain, which is exactly why it's so often missed. Noticing a combination of these signs is a reasonable prompt to look at meal composition, movement, sleep, and stress, and to loop in your doctor if the pattern persists or risk factors are present.
